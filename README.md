---

I build and evaluate measurement models — mostly for large-scale educational assessment, where the question is usually some version of *"does this test mean the same thing for everyone taking it?"* My work sits at the intersection of item response theory, invariance testing, and network psychometrics, and everything I publish is meant to be re-runnable from source.

I'm now a postdoctoral researcher in the Department of Behavioral & Social Sciences at the University of Houston's Fertitta Family College of Medicine, applying that same measurement lens to health and behavioral science data.

Before graduate school I taught science and mathematics in Türkiye and managed STEM programs for the Ministry of National Education, which is still where most of my substantive questions come from.

---

## 🔬 Research focus

- **Item response theory & CAT** — parameter estimation, model–data fit, adaptive test design
- **Measurement invariance** — full and partial invariance, alignment, cross-group and cross-cultural comparability
- **Network psychometrics** — bias-aware estimation of psychological networks
- **SEM & multilevel modeling** — latent variable models for nested, large-scale data
- **Bayesian methods in measurement** — regularization, shrinkage, and priors for sparse predictor sets
- **Large-scale assessment** — PIRLS, TIMSS, and other international survey data; equating and scaling

## 🎓 Dissertation

***Bias-Aware Network Psychometrics*** — Baylor University, 2026.
Methods for estimating psychological networks when measurement bias is present rather than assumed away, with simulation evidence on how invariance violations distort recovered network structure.

## 🚧 Currently working on

- Papers drawn from the dissertation, extending the bias-aware framework to applied assessment data
- Packaging the dissertation simulation code as a documented, installable R workflow
- Teaching notes on SEM (models, fit, interpretation) and applied IRT
